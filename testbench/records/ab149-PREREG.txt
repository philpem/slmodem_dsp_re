PRE-REGISTRATION -- #149 bad-block-run threshold, bench A/B
Written 2026-08-13 before any call of this batch was placed.

WHY PRE-REGISTER.  Nine mechanisms have been proposed in this investigation
and seven withdrawn, several of them mine, and two were withdrawn only because
a metric chosen after seeing the data turned out to be p=0.05 post-hoc on n=9
(1945).  The outcome, the window and the exclusion rule are fixed here, before
the data exists, so that cannot happen again.

THE CHANGE UNDER TEST
  v34hshak.c:10096, gated on DSPLIB_V34_RRN_ON_BADBLOCK:

      if (dsplib_v34_rrn_on_badblock
          && !(T3C_RX(obj)->flags & 0x40)
          && dp_rxget(obj, DP_RX_BAD) <= (short)((int)obj->baud_rate * 3))
              dp_rxput(obj, DP_RX_BAD, 0);

  It raises the FULL RETRAIN's threshold above the §11.6 renegotiation's, so
  the cheap recovery (2-3 s) gets first refusal and the expensive one (~10 s)
  becomes the fallback.  Today the ladder is inverted: the retrain fires at
  DP_RX_BAD > baud_rate>>1 and the renegotiation at > 2*baud_rate, so the
  expensive response needs a QUARTER of the evidence the cheap one does
  (1933/1934).  The far end's own request (flag 0x40) is exempt and still
  retrains immediately.

ARMS         control  DSPLIB_V34_RRN_ON_BADBLOCK=0   (ladder as it ships)
             treat    DSPLIB_V34_RRN_ON_BADBLOCK=1

DESIGN       6 calls per arm per far end, far ends 1901 (SupraExpress) and
             1902 (USR Courier) = 24 calls, 12 per arm.
             INTERLEAVED control/treat within each far end, so any drift over
             the batch (line, ATA, thermal, time of night) hits both arms
             equally.  Batch order is fixed here and not changed afterwards.
             Binary: d-modem-jb for BOTH arms -- it carries JBSTAT, so the
             arms differ by one environment variable and nothing else.
             slmodemd: claude_re/build/hybrid-fit/slmodemd-fit, relinked
             2026-08-13 with tools/hybrid_link.sh (the previous binary was an
             hour older than the source it was supposed to be testing).

PRIMARY OUTCOME (fixed now, one only)
  Handshakes in a FIXED 45 s window from the first "V34 bulk delay
  estimation", counted from the BLOB's own string, on calls with >=45 s of
  carrier.  This is 1947's metric and nothing else will be substituted.
  Prediction if the change works: treat < control.

SECONDARY, reported but not decisive
  * final rx bit rate, from V34DATARATE "finally ... rxbitrate", span-matched
  * connect success (the 1932 regression showed up here, not in rates)
  * jbuf lost+empty per second of carrier, from JBSTAT

THE KEYSTONE, same calls, no extra bench time
  row.sh now writes a BENCHANCHOR line giving wall clock at slmodemd's start,
  so slmodemd's relative <t> stamps and pjmedia's wall-clock JBSTAT stamps can
  finally be put on one timeline.  Question: do jitter-buffer underruns
  cluster before retrains, against the call's own base rate as the null?
  A ratio near 1 means underruns are simply common and any clustering is an
  artefact -- which is how the tap-drift and overshoot hypotheses died (1918).

EXCLUSIONS, decided now
  * Any call where the machine was not quiet is DISCARDED, not analysed.  The
    batch gates on this before every call.  A call placed with a game running
    returned CONNECT 4800 against 31200 quiet on the same far end (1951).
  * Calls with <45 s of carrier are excluded from the primary outcome (the
    window cannot be counted) but ARE counted in connect success.
  * Rate is read from the .dte capture, never from the .origin.log -- slmodemd
    logs "result: 1 (CONNECT)" and drops the speed, which once made a working
    sweep look like total failure.

WHAT WOULD MAKE ME ABANDON THE CHANGE
  Connect success materially below control.  1932's attempt gave 1 connect in
  4; that is the failure mode to watch, and it is checked FIRST, before any
  rate or handshake number is looked at.
