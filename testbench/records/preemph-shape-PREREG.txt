PRE-REGISTRATION -- shape-matched pre-emphasis, scored on equaliser workload
Written 2026-08-14, before the code had ever been run.

THE CHANGE UNDER TEST
  probe_preemp_shape() in V34hshak.c, gated DSPLIB_V34_SHAPE_PREEMP.
  Scores all eleven V.34 transmit-spectrum templates against the measured probe
  bins and returns the best fit, instead of reducing the channel to a two-point
  tilt and indexing a counter that can only reach 6-10.  Findings F1956, F1957.
  Committed 10dbf7c, make phase green, never executed.

THE OUTCOME, and why it is not the connect rate
  Phil's proposal, and it is better than what the previous A/B used: *"compare
  equaliser coefficients / frequency shape, less error amplitude per bin should
  indicate the equaliser is doing less work."*

  A pre-emphasis filter that matches the channel leaves the equaliser with
  nothing to do.  One that does not leaves the equaliser correcting the
  shortfall itself.  So the equaliser's workload measures the pre-emphasis
  choice far more directly than a bit rate does -- and the bit rate on this
  bench varies by thousands of bits per second call to call, which is a noise
  floor no 0.13 dB effect can be seen through.

  PRIMARY:   off-centre / centre tap-energy ratio, from V34EQTAPS.
             A perfectly matched channel needs the centre tap and nothing else,
             so off-centre energy IS the work.  The RATIO, not the absolutes:
             absolute tap magnitudes move with the transmit level exactly as
             equerr does (V34TXSCALE at the phase 3->4 boundary).
             Taken over blocks in DATA mode only, after the last handshake.
  SECONDARY: equerr from the same V34EQTAPS lines; the index actually chosen
             (V34PREEMPHASIS SHAPE vs the object's line); connect rate.

ARMS         control  DSPLIB_V34_SHAPE_PREEMP=0   (object's counter, 6-10 only)
             treat    DSPLIB_V34_SHAPE_PREEMP=1   (all 11, shape matched)
             BOTH with DSPLIB_V34_DUMP_EQ_TAPS=1, so the arms differ by exactly
             one environment variable and both produce the primary outcome.

DESIGN       6 per arm per far end, 1901 and 1902, interleaved, quiet machine
             gated before every call.  ab149.sh with TAG=pesh REPS=6.
             12 per arm is not proposed: see the expectation below -- a bigger
             sample does not rescue an effect this small, and the bench time is
             better spent on #152.

*** THE EXPECTED RESULT IS A NULL, AND THAT IS NOT A FAILURE ***

  Finding F1956 measured the whole prize on this path at 0.13 dB RMS residual
  (object index 7 at 1.37 dB, best available index 9 at 1.24 dB).  The reason is
  measured, not guessed: this channel is flat to +/-0.4 dB from 450 to 3150 Hz,
  so there is almost no tilt for any template to correct, and its one real
  defect is a band-edge cliff -- -18.3 dB at 3450 Hz -- against which the largest
  template offers 7.5 dB.  The dominant impairment is beyond the authority of
  the entire mechanism.

  So the prediction is NO MEASURABLE CHANGE in the tap-energy ratio.  If that is
  what comes back, it CONFIRMS 1956 and the correct write-up is "the defect is
  real, the fix is correct, and this bench cannot see it".  It must not be
  re-scored, re-windowed or re-armed until something moves.  That is exactly
  what happened to the first ladder A/B (1955), and the lesson is cheaper the
  second time.

  A MEASURABLE DROP in off-centre energy would be a genuine surprise and would
  mean 1956's residual model is missing something -- most likely the template
  breakpoints, which are read off a figure drawn with +/-1 dB tolerance and are
  the weakest part of the model.  Chase that before believing the improvement.

  A MEASURABLE RISE would mean the shape matcher is choosing worse than the
  counter, which would point at the 0.4-0.8 interpolation (unconstrained by the
  Recommendation, linear by my choice) or at the variance-vs-mean-square
  scoring.  Both are in template_db() and probe_preemp_shape() respectively.

BEFORE THE FIRST CALL, three checks that cost no bench time
  1. Relink: tools/hybrid_link.sh, never by hand.  The bench binary was once an
     hour older than the source it was supposed to be testing.
  2. One EMULATED call with the flag set, to confirm the selector fires at all
     and prints "V34PREEMPHASIS, - SHAPE index N ... var X.XX dB2", and that
     V34EQTAPS appears once per 1024-symbol block.
  3. Cross-check the C against the Python: run testbench/preemphshape.py on that
     capture and confirm both pick the SAME index.  They implement the same
     eleven templates independently, so a disagreement means one is wrong, and
     finding out on the bench would waste calls.
