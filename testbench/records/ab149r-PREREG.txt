PRE-REGISTRATION -- #149 bad-block-run threshold, REPLICATION
Written 2026-08-14 before any call of this batch was placed.

WHY A REPLICATION.  The first batch (records/ab149-PREREG.txt, finding 1953)
gave p=0.036 on its pre-registered outcome and the change looked settled.
Re-scoring it with outcomes that exclude fewer calls -- chosen to maximise
RETENTION, not effect -- gave p=0.090 and p=0.085 (finding 1955).  The smallest
p came from the outcome that discarded the most data, which is the signature of
a fragile result.  The effect SIZE was consistent across all three scorings (the
median handshake count roughly halves), but that is equally what a lenient
metric on a small sample looks like when there is no effect.  This batch exists
to tell those apart.

WHAT CHANGED IN THE DESIGN, and why

  1. PRIMARY OUTCOME IS NOW HANDSHAKES PER MINUTE OF CARRIER, on every call
     that reached carrier at all.  ZERO EXCLUSION.
     The old outcome required 45 s of carrier before a call could be scored,
     which threw away 13 of 23 calls and -- worse -- conditioned on call
     duration, something the treatment can itself affect.  That is a collider,
     and pre-registering it stopped it being a fishing expedition without
     stopping it being a bias.  Retention should have been the design criterion
     from the start.

  2. SAMPLE DOUBLED to 12 per arm per far end = 24 per arm, 48 calls.
     At the effect size observed (5.28 -> 2.58 handshakes/min) this should
     resolve either way rather than landing at p ~ 0.085 again.

  3. CONNECT SUCCESS IS PROMOTED TO A CO-PRIMARY, not a gate.
     The first batch gave 9/12 treatment against 11/12 control.  Not
     significant at n=12, not the 1-in-4 collapse of the earlier attempt, but
     the direction is unfavourable and n=24 per arm can actually measure it.
     A change that halves retrains and costs connects is not obviously a win.

UNCHANGED FROM THE FIRST BATCH, deliberately, so the two are comparable:
  arms      control DSPLIB_V34_RRN_ON_BADBLOCK=0, treat =1
  far ends  1901 (SupraExpress) and 1902 (USR Courier)
  order     arms INTERLEAVED within each far end
  binary    d-modem-jb for BOTH arms; one environment variable is the only
            difference between them
  slmodemd  claude_re/build/hybrid-fit/slmodemd-fit
  harness   testbench/ab149.sh, the SAME script, parameterised by REPS and TAG
            rather than copied -- a second copy would drift and the comparison
            would quietly stop being one
  gating    quiet machine required before EVERY call, not just the first

SECONDARY, reported but not decisive
  * handshakes in a fixed 15 s window (kept for comparability with 1955)
  * final rx bit rate, span-matched
  * jbuf lost+empty per second of carrier

ANALYSIS, fixed now
  Exact permutation test, one-tailed (the prediction is directional: treatment
  fewer handshakes than control), on handshakes per minute.  Connect success by
  Fisher exact, two-tailed -- there is no directional prediction there and a
  gain would be as interesting as a loss.

WHAT WOULD MAKE ME ABANDON THE CHANGE
  * connect success significantly below control, or
  * handshakes/min not lower in treatment at p < 0.05 with n=24 per arm.
    At this sample size a null is a real answer, not an underpowered one, and
    it should be reported as such rather than re-scored until something moves.

WHAT THIS BATCH CANNOT DO
  Settle whether the RATE improves.  Rate is confounded by far end (1902 sits
  at 28800 in both arms with no headroom) and by call duration.  Handshake rate
  is the mechanism under test; rate is downstream of it and of much else.
