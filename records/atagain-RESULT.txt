RESULT -- VG204 output attenuation 3 dB on port 0/1 (ext 1902, USR Courier)
Run 2026-08-16 02:24-02:40 on a quiet machine.  Pre-registration: atagain-PREREG.txt

*** CORRECTION, ADDED AFTER THE CALLS: THE CHANGE WAS A NO-OP ***

  `show running-config all` on voice-port 0/1 afterwards reads
  `output attenuation 3` -- and 3 dB is this platform's DEFAULT.  The effective
  value was 3 before and 3 after.  Nothing was applied.  Everything below is a
  measurement of an unchanged system, which is exactly why it reproduced the
  archive so precisely.

  The failure mode was named in advance, in the advice that accompanied the
  commands: "read the current value before you set it -- `output attenuation`
  is an absolute, not a delta.  If it's already 3, setting 3 changes nothing
  and you'll chase a null."  Six bench calls then went out without that read
  being confirmed.  A precondition that is stated but not GATED ON is not a
  precondition.

  So: "does attenuating the ATA change what the far end asks for" is UNTESTED,
  not answered.  A retry must use a value that differs from 3 -- and, per the
  spread below, differs by a lot.

THE PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTION IS NOT MET.

PRIMARY -- the Courier's requested power reduction
  9 handshakes across 3 calls:   3  3  3  9  3  6  3  3  9
  Archived at 0 dB, 121 handshakes:   3 x65   6 x51   9 x5   -- never below 3
  Prediction was "falls below 3, ideally 0-1".  It did not, on any handshake.
  The distribution is indistinguishable from the archive.

SECONDARY -- our transmit level
  -24.29, -24.29, -24.16 dBFS.  Archived median for a 3 dB request: -24.30.
  Identical.  Nothing moved.

CONTROL -- 1901 Supra, port 0/0, untouched, same binary, same session
  11 handshakes:  2 1 1 1 2  /  2 1 2 2 2 3
  Archived: 1 x65  2 x86  3 x2  6 x1.  Identical.
  txRMS -22.24 dBFS against an archived -22.75/-22.99.  Identical.
  So nothing global moved, and the 1902 null is not masking a bench-wide shift.
  (The first control call handshook five times and never settled -- recorded,
  not diagnosed; port 0/0 was untouched so it is unrelated to this test.  The
  second connected at 4800.)

*** BUT THE TEST COULD NOT HAVE DETECTED THE EFFECT IT WAS LOOKING FOR ***

  ATI11's `Recv/Xmit Level (-dB)` on the three 1902 calls -- the far end's own
  report of the analog level reaching it, and the only witness to whether the
  gateway change did anything at all:

      call 1   20/18        call 2   20/19        call 3   25/18

  FIVE dB of spread across three identical calls.  A 3 dB step is inside it.
  The pre-registration's claim that "n=1 is adequate because the quantity is
  near-deterministic per far end" was WRONG on both outcomes: the request is
  bimodal 3/6, not deterministic, and the level wanders 5 dB.

  So this run does not distinguish "the mechanism is wrong" from "the config
  did not take effect".  Both remain live.  Do not read the null as a
  refutation of finding 1966's model; read it as a test with too little power.

*** AND THE WHOLE LINE IS AIMED AT THE DIRECTION THAT ALREADY WORKS ***

  ATI11, same calls:   Speed 28800/12000     Recv/Xmit Level 20/18

  The Courier RECEIVES us at -20 dBm -- a healthy level -- and gets 28800 out
  of it.  We receive the Courier, transmitting at -18 dBm, and manage 12000.

  `output attenuation` on port 0/1 changes only what the FAR END hears.  That
  is the 28800 direction.  It cannot touch the 12000 direction at all.  So even
  if the knob works perfectly and finding 1966's model is exactly right, the
  best available outcome is a fraction of a dB of margin on the direction that
  is not the problem.

  That is a defect in the RECOMMENDATION, not in the execution, and it was
  visible in principle before any call was placed.  It is the reason to stop
  here rather than to spend more bench time on a bigger step.

RECOMMENDATION
  Revert `voice-port 0/1 / output attenuation 3` to the default.  No benefit
  was demonstrated, none is available in the direction that matters, and it is
  a live gateway.

  If certainty about the knob is wanted later for its own sake, the test is a
  12 dB step -- unambiguous against a 5 dB spread -- and three calls reading
  ATI11.  That answers "does the control work", which is worth knowing, but it
  does not resurrect the throughput argument.

WHAT THIS RUN DID PRODUCE, and it is worth more than the null
  ATI11 on the Courier gives `Recv/Xmit Level` and `Speed` per direction in one
  line.  No archived capture had it -- every &V1 block in the archive is Supra
  or Oli'Net, and the Courier needs ATI11 rather than &V1 (modems.sh already
  knew; row.sh does not read it).  It is the per-direction level and rate
  instrument this bench has been missing, and $CLAUDE_JOB_DIR/atacall.sh has
  the two-line recipe.  Fold it into row.sh.
