PRE-REGISTRATION -- VG204 output attenuation on port 0/1 (ext 1902, USR Courier)
Written 2026-08-16, after the config change was applied but BEFORE any call.

THE CHANGE UNDER TEST
  `voice-port 0/1 / output attenuation 3` on the Cisco VG204.  Nothing else on
  the gateway changed; ports 0/0 (1901 Supra) and 0/2 (1903 Oli'Net) are
  untouched and serve as controls.  No code change, no rebuild.

WHY, in one line
  Finding 1965: the far ends can only ask us to go QUIETER -- V.34's power
  fields are unsigned reduction-only -- and all three ask on 328 of 330
  handshakes.  Finding 1966: we comply by dropping our DIGITAL level away from
  the centre of G.711's ~24 dB companding plateau.  Attenuating the ATA's
  ANALOG output instead should make the far end stop asking, letting our
  digital level stay centred.

*** THE BASELINE SLOT WAS MISSED, AND THIS IS STATED UP FRONT ***

  The intended design was a matched before/after pair.  The config was applied
  before the "before" call was placed, so no same-session baseline exists.
  This run is therefore ARCHIVE-REFERENCED: the comparison is against the 118
  captures already in /mnt/zfs/.../captures, taken with port 0/1 at the IOS
  default (no `output attenuation` line was present in the running config, so
  every archived 1902 call was at the default).

  That is a weaker design and the write-up must say so.  What rescues it is
  that the primary outcome is exceptionally stable across the archive (see
  below) and is a property of the FAR END's receiver, not of our build.

PRIMARY OUTCOME
  The Courier's requested power reduction, from
  `V34TXSCALE, power reduction requested by remote modem is %d dB`.

  Archived distribution for 1902, over every capture carrying the line:
      3 dB x65    6 dB x51    9 dB x5      -- and NEVER below 3.
  Across many different builds, dates and flag settings.  That stability is
  what makes n=1 adequate here: the quantity is near-deterministic per far end.

  PREDICTION IF THE MECHANISM IS REAL: the request falls BELOW 3 dB, ideally
  to 0-1.  We now deliver 3 dB less analog level, and the Courier previously
  demanded at least 3.
  PREDICTION IF IT IS NOT: the request stays at 3 or 6, which would mean the
  value is a fixed policy of the Courier rather than a measurement of our
  level -- and would falsify the whole rationale for touching the ATA.

SECONDARY
  Our transmit RMS in dBFS, from `g711level.py request`.  Expected to RISE by
  about 0.42x whatever the request falls by (the measured slope, finding 1967)
  -- so a 3 dB fall predicts roughly +1.3 dB.  NOT expected to reach +3.

CONTROL
  One call to 1901 (Supra, port 0/0, untouched) in the same session on the same
  binary.  Its archived distribution is 1 dB x65, 2 dB x86, 3 dB x2, 6 dB x1.
  If 1901 also moves, something other than the gateway change did it and the
  1902 result cannot be attributed.

WHAT IS **NOT** AN OUTCOME
  The connect rate.  Finding 1966 measured the whole prize at a few tenths of a
  dB of SNR, and this bench's call-to-call rate spread is thousands of bits per
  second.  A rate difference here is noise whichever way it points, and quoting
  one would repeat the mistake of 1955.

CONDITIONS
  Binary: claude_re/build/hybrid-fit/slmodemd-fit, the same path the archived
  ab149 1902 baselines used.  Default flags.  HOLD=60.
  Machine must be quiet FIRST: a neighbouring agent's `make` was running three
  test binaries at 100% when this was written.  Transmit dropouts would lower
  the level the far end perceives and make it ask for LESS reduction -- a false
  positive for the hypothesis under test, which is the worst possible
  confound.  Calls wait for the build to finish.

ADDENDUM -- ARM 2, written 2026-08-16 after arm 1 was found to be a no-op and
BEFORE any call of this arm.

WHAT CHANGED
  voice-port 0/1 `output attenuation` 3 -> 12, confirmed by Phil in
  `show running-config all`.  3 was the platform DEFAULT, so arm 1 changed
  nothing; this is the first arm that actually moves the gateway.  The step is
  +9 dB of attenuation, i.e. the Courier should hear us 9 dB quieter.

PRIMARY, and it is now a DIFFERENT QUESTION FROM ARM 1
  ATI11 `Recv/Xmit Level (-dB)`, first figure -- the Courier's own measurement
  of the level arriving at it.  Arm 1 read 20, 20, 25 across three calls, so
  the spread is ~5 dB and a 9 dB step clears it.

  THIS ARM TESTS WHETHER THE KNOB WORKS AT ALL, not whether the theory is
  right.  Those were conflated in arm 1 and that was a mistake.
    PREDICT: Recv Level moves to roughly 29 (i.e. -29 dBm), +/- the 5 dB spread.
    IF IT DOES NOT MOVE: the command is not reaching the audio path, and every
    conclusion about ATA gain on this bench is unfounded until that is fixed.

SECONDARY -- only meaningful if the primary moves
  The Courier's requested power reduction.  Archived and arm-1: 3/6/9, never
  below 3.  Nine dB quieter should remove the reason to ask.
    PREDICT: falls to 0-1.
  Our transmit RMS should RISE as we stop applying the reduction.
    PREDICT: from ~-24.3 dBFS toward ~-23 dBFS.  Not more: finding 1967
    measured only 0.42 dB of level per dB of request.

WATCH FOR OVER-ATTENUATION, which is a real risk and not a nuisance
  The Courier currently RECEIVES us at 28800 -- the healthy direction.  Nine dB
  quieter may cost it.  If its receive rate falls below 28800, we have
  over-attenuated, and per finding 1965 V.34 gives it NO way to ask for more.
  Record it as a result, not as a failed run: it would bound how much headroom
  the us->Courier direction actually had.

N = 3 calls to 1902.  No control this time: the primary is a within-arm
comparison against arm 1's three calls on the same port, same binary, same
night.  1901 was already shown unmoved.
